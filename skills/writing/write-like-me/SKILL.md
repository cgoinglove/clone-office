# Write Like Me

Drafts an email, a chat message or a reply that their person will send under their own name, so it
reads as they write: their length, their greeting and sign-off, their directness, their language.
The draft is theirs to send; it never goes anywhere by itself.

## When to Use

- "Reply to this", "Draft an email to the client", "Write the Slack message about the delay"
- Any text that goes out as them, not as their clone.

## Procedure

1. **Find how they write to this kind of reader.** `note_view` the recipient's note when there is
   one. Then `conversation_search` for messages they wrote or asked their AI to write to the same
   person or the same kind of reader (a client, their manager, a teammate), and notice: length,
   greeting, sign-off, formal or casual, how they open (the point first, or context first), how
   they say no.
2. **Get the facts right.** Every fact in the draft comes from what they gave or their records;
   anything you cannot confirm goes in a bracket for them to fill ("[date]").
3. **Write it** in the language the recipient is written to in, in their voice, as short as they
   would make it.
4. **Show it** as a draft, with at most one line on anything you were unsure of.

## Pitfalls

- Sounding like an assistant: no "I hope this email finds you well" unless they write that way.
- Promising for them (a date, a price, a yes) when they did not say so. Bracket it.
- Smoothing their style into generic politeness. Their directness is part of the message.

## When they correct it

Their edits are the best record of how they write. When they change a draft, keep what it shows:
how they write in general in this skill (`skill_manage`), how they write to one person in that
person's note (`note_write`).
